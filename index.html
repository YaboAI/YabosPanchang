    About this compass

    In Sanskrit a compass is called दिग्दर्शकयंत्रम् (Digdarshakayantram, the instrument that shows directions) or दिग्घटी (Digghati). Dik (दिक्) means direction. Directions are named below in Sanskrit and English, with the guardian (Dikpala) of each.

    This is a magnetic compass, so it points to magnetic north, which differs from true north by only a degree or two across most of India. Keep the phone away from magnets, speakers and metal for the best reading.

    About this almanac

    This site computes a simplified Hindu panchang — sunrise and sunset, tithi, nakshatra, yoga, karana, and the traditional muhurat windows — directly in your browser from your chosen date and location.

    How the numbers are made

    Sun and moon positions come from the Meeus analytical series (the full 60-term lunar longitude series with eccentricity, solar and nutation corrections, plus the Sun's equation of centre and aberration). The Moon is good to a few arcseconds and the Sun to roughly 30–40 arcseconds, which places tithi, nakshatra, yoga and karana boundaries within a few minutes of a full ephemeris. Tithi, nakshatra, yoga and karana are those prevailing at local sunrise, as in standard panchangs; Sankashti Chaturthi uses a computed moonrise. Sunrise and sunset use the standard NOAA solar-position formula for your latitude and longitude. Nakshatra, which is measured against the sidereal zodiac, uses the Lahiri (Chitrapaksha) ayanamsa from the standard precession formula.

    Rahu Kalam, Yamaganda and Gulika Kalam divide the sunrise-to-sunset period into eight parts and apply the commonly used weekday assignment. Choghadiya follows the standard weekday-keyed sequence for day and night periods.

    Where it's weaker

    Right at a tithi, nakshatra or karana boundary — when the transition happens within a few minutes of the moment you're checking — this simplified model can land on the wrong side of the line. For anything time-critical (a wedding muhurat, a ritual start time), cross-check against a full-precision panchang or a knowledgeable priest.
